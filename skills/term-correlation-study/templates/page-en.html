Written work predicts results. Attendance, on its own, does not. Reading it honestly

What this does and does not show

It is an early warning, not just a summary

Rebuilt using only homework settled before each exam, the link holds. A slipping homework record predicts the next test — it is visible in advance, not only in hindsight.

Homework and assignments are one thing, not two

The two submission rates move together almost perfectly. Treat them as a single “does this child do written work at home” signal rather than two separate levers.

The same teacher judges both

Homework quality and test marks come from the same hand, so some of that link may be one teacher’s view of a child showing up twice. Plain submission — a clerical fact — still predicts strongly on its own.

It is one term and 66 children

This shows that diligent children score well. It does not prove that pushing a reluctant child into submitting homework will raise their marks. Use it to spot children, not to justify pressure.

Leave applications are effectively unused

{{headline.absenceDays}} absence-days this term; {{headline.leaveCovered}} had an approved leave application behind them. So the system cannot currently tell an authorised absence from an unexplained one.

Three children have stopped coming

Present 2, 8 and 14 days out of 60 — all still active on the roll, all still being issued homework, none with a leave application. They quietly distort every class average.

What to do

Five things that follow from this

  1. Set the attendance alert at 85%, not 95%.Moving a child from 95% to 98% buys nothing measurable. The children below 85% are the ones falling behind.
  2. Treat a slipping homework record as the early warning.It is the strongest signal in the data and it is visible before the test, especially in Classes 3 to 5.
  3. Watch the chase counter.Children chased repeatedly for missing work score markedly lower. Repeated chasing is a symptom worth escalating, not a routine step.
  4. In Class 4 and Class 5, treat absence as academic.These are the classes nearest the scholarship exam, and the only ones where attendance clearly tracks results.
  5. Resolve the three long-term absentees.Either bring them back or take them off roll — and stop issuing homework nobody can submit.

Method. Attendance is read through the app’s own unit roll-up, so Classes 1–5 are counted from the Quran-group capture taken in the first period and Nursery/KG from the section capture — the same figures the attendance reports show. Homework and assignment “submitted” means a record reaching SUBMITTED, CHECKED, RESUBMIT or RETURNED; “not submitted” means it sat in CHASE or DUE. Records in ABSENT_REDELIVER are excluded from both, since they describe an absence rather than a choice. Quality scores CORRECT as 1, PARTIAL as 0.5, WRONG as 0.

Results are a child’s average percentage across every printed class test they sat; cancelled tests and papers marked ABSENT are excluded. Every finding was re-checked against a version that standardises each score within its own test, removing test difficulty and class ability — the written-work findings survive that, and so does the absence of an attendance effect.
